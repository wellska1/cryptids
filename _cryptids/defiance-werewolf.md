Long before anyone agreed on what was seen, Defiance had already chosen the name: the werewolf by the tracks.

In the summer of 1972, Defiance felt like the kind of town where every sound traveled. A whistle from the rail line could roll across back yards, and a rumor could do the same by morning. Then came reports of a wolf-headed figure near the tracks, and suddenly porch conversations turned into late-night stakeouts and flashlight patrols.

The story moved fast because it had all the right ingredients: a dark corridor of railroad right-of-way, a shape seen at the edge of a beam, and witnesses who were certain they had seen something no one could easily name. Local coverage gave the tale a pulse, and every retelling added one more detail about eyes in the brush, heavy footsteps, or a silhouette that stood too tall for a dog and too wild for a man.
